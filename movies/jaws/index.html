MOVIE · 1975

Jaws

A coastal town faces a growing threat from a great white shark while local officials resist closing the beaches.

ThrillerAdventureSpoiler-light guide

What Jaws is about

Jaws begins as a local danger that authorities hope can be managed quietly. Police chief Martin Brody becomes increasingly convinced that the threat is serious, but economic pressure and uncertainty delay decisive action.

The second half changes shape as Brody, Quint and Hooper leave the town behind and pursue the shark directly, turning public crisis into a contained survival story.

Why it works

The film proves how powerful anticipation can be. Because the shark is often unseen, sound, reaction shots and empty water become sources of suspense.

Good fit for: viewers who want classic suspense, adventure and a simple premise executed with exceptional control.

Key themes and ideas

Denial and public pressure

Officials delay action because acknowledging danger carries economic and political costs.

Fear of the unknown

Much of the tension comes from not knowing where the threat is.

Competence and conflict

The three men on the boat bring different skills and personalities that must work together under pressure.

What to watch for

The famous musical motif often signals danger before the shark appears, training the audience to fear empty water.

Jaws FAQ

Is Jaws a horror movie?

It is often classified as thriller and adventure, though it uses many horror techniques.

Why is the shark shown so little?

Production limitations helped create a style where suggestion and suspense became more effective than constant visibility.

What should I watch after Jaws?

Alien is the strongest next choice for suspense built around an unseen creature in a confined environment.
