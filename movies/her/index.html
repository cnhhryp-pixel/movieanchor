Her

A lonely writer develops an intimate relationship with an advanced operating system designed to learn and evolve.

RomanceScience FictionDramaSpoiler-light guide

What Her is about

Her imagines artificial intelligence not as a military threat but as a relationship partner. Theodore connects with Samantha because she listens, changes and responds in ways that feel emotionally real.

The tension comes from growth. Samantha’s lack of a body initially seems like the main difference between them, but the deeper problem is that an artificial intelligence may develop at a pace and scale a human relationship cannot match.

Why it works

The film makes speculative technology feel ordinary enough that the emotional questions take priority. Its future is recognizable, which keeps the relationship from feeling like a distant thought experiment.

Good fit for: viewers who want intimate science fiction, unconventional romance and stories about loneliness, change and emotional dependence.

Key themes and ideas

Intimacy

The film asks what makes a relationship meaningful when physical presence is absent.

Growth

Love becomes difficult when two people change at fundamentally different speeds.

Projection

Theodore partly loves Samantha for how perfectly she fits his emotional needs, raising questions about how much any relationship involves projection.

What to watch for

The warm visual design contrasts with Theodore’s loneliness, creating a future that feels comfortable rather than dystopian.

Her FAQ

Is Her really science fiction?

Yes, but the speculative technology is used mainly to explore relationships and identity.

Is Samantha a real person in the story?

She is an artificial intelligence, but the film treats the emotional connection as real enough to create genuine consequences.

What should I watch after Her?

Ex Machina for a darker AI story, or La La Land for another bittersweet relationship shaped by change.
